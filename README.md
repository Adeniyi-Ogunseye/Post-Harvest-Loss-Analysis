Project Title: A Descriptive Analysis of Post-harvest Grain Losses Across Nigerian Supply Chains

Domain: Agriculture

Project Objective
Understanding the importance of post-harvest losses – and specifically why addressing and reducing them matters – is critical for global food security, economic stability, and resource conservation.
This project sees to quantify, visualize, and map the economic and volume losses of key staple grains in Nigeria in order to identify which regions, crops, and supply chain stages experience the highest inefficiencies to help policymakers target interventions.

Selected Crops
The focus will be the top three cereal grains produced in Nigeria which are maize, rice, and sorghum.

Data Source
The foundational, source-level raw datasets from which these comprehensive national and sub-national agricultural metrics are compiled can be accessed through their primary institutional portals:
National Production & Household Surveys: The comprehensive sub-national household and crop data is hosted on the National Bureau of Statistics Microdata Catalog under the National Agricultural Sample Census (NASC) portal.

Macro Production & Yield Metrics: National tracking balances across multiple decades for rice, maize, and sorghum are available via the FAO in Nigeria Core Statistics Dashboard.

Post-Harvest Loss Matrices: The regional, crop-specific algorithm structures and processing stage weight percentages are compiled on the APHLIS Tables for Dry Weight Losses in Nigeria. 

State-Level Loss Studies: Academic value-chain loss models and state-by-state localized parameters are sourced directly through the active peer-reviewed database on the ResearchGate Agriculture Network.

Tasks
1. Key Metrics to Calculate

Loss Percentage: (Volume Lost / Total Volume Harvested) × 100

Financial Impact: Volume Lost × Average Local Market Price (expressed in Nigerian Naira ₦)

Caloric Deficit: Volume Lost × Calorie Count per Kilogram (to show the impact on food security)


2. Analytical Dimensions (How to slice the data)

By Crop Type: Maize, Rice, Sorghum.

By Supply Chain Stage: Pinpoint where losses happen—harvesting, storage, transport, or processing.

By Geography: Aggregate data by geopolitical zones to find regional hotspots.
