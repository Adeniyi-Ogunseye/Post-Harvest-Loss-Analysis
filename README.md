Project Title: A Descriptive Analysis of Post-harvest Grain Losses Across Nigerian Supply Chains

Domain: Agriculture

Project Objective
Understanding the importance of post-harvest losses – and specifically why addressing and reducing them matters – is critical for global food security, economic stability, and resource conservation.
This project sees to quantify, visualize, and map the economic and volume losses of key staple grains in Nigeria in order to identify which regions, crops, and supply chain stages experience the highest inefficiencies to help policymakers target interventions.
Selected Crops
The focus will be the top three cereal grains produced in Nigeria which are maize, rice, and sorghum.
Data Source

1.	I did a search on Gemini for “dataset on production figures for rice, maize, sorghum in Nigeria 2010 to 2025”
2.	I asked the AI to “include production figures for each state over the same period which can be downloaded”
3.	I then asked the AI to “include figures for post-harvest losses for each state by supply chain stage”
4.	I further asked the AI to “include details like volume lost, total volume harvested, average local market price (in Naira), caloric count per kg.”
5.	The generated dataset, “Nigeria Crop Comprehensive Metrics 2010-2025” was then downloaded.







Tasks

1. Key Metrics to Calculate

Loss Percentage: (Volume Lost / Total Volume Harvested) × 100

Financial Impact: Volume Lost × Average Local Market Price (expressed in Nigerian Naira ₦)

Caloric Deficit: Volume Lost × Calorie Count per Kilogram (to show the impact on food security)


2. Analytical Dimensions (How to slice the data)

By Crop Type: Maize, Rice, Sorghum.

By Supply Chain Stage: Pinpoint where losses happen—harvesting, storage, transport, or processing.

By Geography: Aggregate data by geopolitical zones to find regional hotspots.


Data Limitations
The loss rate for each crop, Maize (22%), Sorghum (17%) and Rice (15%) irrespective of state or year is fixed as well as the split of losses across supply chain stages for each crop (e.g. maize storage loss is always 45.5% of maize losses). This means:
1.	“Crop losses increase as production increases” is guaranteed by the data, because loss is a fixed percentage of harvest.
2.	The region loss rates (17.71% to 19.12%) differ only because regions grow a different mix of crops. Regions that produce more harvest for specific crops show higher loss rates for the same crop.
3.	The loss rates attributed to the supply chain stages restates an assumption built into the dataset.
The data appears to be modelled from APHLIS (African Postharvest Losses Information System) average loss percentages rather than measured state by state.
